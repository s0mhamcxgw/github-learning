## Bilingual narration (contestant reads the English part aloud and records it themselves, Chinese is only used as subtitles)

1. What does China look like, told not in words, but in data? I study big data — and these are the numbers I believe the world should see.
   Without using words, but using data to tell it, what does China look like? I study big data. These, I believe, are the numbers the world should see.
2. In 2015, renewable energy was still a small share of China's power mix. Coal wrote most of the story then.
   In 2015, renewable energy still occupied only a small part of China's power structure. The story then was mainly written by coal.
3. Then the curve began to climb — year after year, steeper and faster, a growth line rarely seen in energy history.
   Then, the curve began to climb. Year after year, steeper and faster — a growth curve rarely seen in energy history.
4. By the end of 2025, China's solar capacity reached 1.2 billion kilowatts, up 35 percent in a single year — the largest solar fleet on Earth.
   By the end of 2025, China's solar installed capacity reached 1.2 billion kilowatts, growing 35% in one year — the largest solar array on Earth.
5. On grasslands and coastlines, wind turbines added up to 640 million kilowatts, turning breezes into a national grid.
   On grasslands and coastlines, wind turbines have accumulated to 640 million kilowatts, turning wind into a national grid.
6. In 2025, wind and solar together passed 1.84 billion kilowatts — overtaking coal-fired power for the first time in history.
   In 2025, wind and solar combined exceeded 1.84 billion kilowatts — surpassing thermal power for the first time in history.
7. Today, renewables make up about 60 percent of China's total installed capacity. Six in every ten kilowatts are green.
   Today, renewable energy accounts for about 60% of China's total installed capacity. Out of every 10 kilowatts, 6 are green.
8. China's renewable generation reached roughly 4 trillion kilowatt-hours a year — more than the electricity use of all 27 EU countries combined.
   China's annual renewable energy generation is about 4 trillion kilowatt-hours — exceeding the total electricity consumption of the 27 EU countries.
9. From rooftops to deserts, solar panels now power villages, factories and cities, from east to west.
   From rooftops to deserts, photovoltaics now power villages, factories, and cities, from east to west.
10. Behind every kilowatt-hour is data: sensors, forecasts and algorithms balancing supply and demand in real time.
    Behind every kilowatt-hour is data: sensors, forecasts, and algorithms, balancing supply and demand in real time.
11. As a big-data student, I see my future in these curves — models that make every watt cleaner and smarter.
    As a big data major student, I see my future in these curves — models that make every watt of electricity cleaner and smarter.
12. Green electrons travel thousands of kilometers, lighting homes far from where the wind blows.
    Green electrons cross thousands of kilometers, lighting up homes in places the wind never reaches.
13. Fewer emissions, bluer skies: the carbon intensity of China's growth keeps falling, year by year.
    Fewer emissions, bluer skies: the carbon intensity of China's growth declines year by year.
14. This is China through data — not a slogan, but a measurable, verifiable transformation. The numbers will keep climbing, and the next chapter of this curve will be written by my generation. China through data: powered by wind and sun, told by us.
    This is China in data — not a slogan, but a measurable, verifiable transformation. The numbers will continue to climb, and the next chapter of this curve will be written by our generation. Telling China through data: powered by wind and sun, told by us.
